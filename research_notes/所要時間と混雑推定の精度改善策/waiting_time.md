# Predicting passenger waiting time and next-bus arrival for low-frequency, irregular bus services (Delhi DTC context)

*Evidence tags: **[S]** = peer-reviewed and/or large automated dataset (AFC/AVL); **[M]** = peer-reviewed but small or specific sample, conference paper, or official agency definition; **[W]** = vendor claim, news, unreviewed GitHub analysis, or seen only as a search-result abstract/snippet (not full text). "Our" numbers refer to the team's own system: historical E[H²]/(2E[H]) model, individual-wait median absolute % error (MdAPE) ≈59%; live-upstream ETA ≈22%; upstream bus visible ≈47% of the time.*

## 1. Conditional waiting time given elapsed time since the last bus (renewal theory / residual life / hazard models)

### Takeaway
The current model predicts the *mean* of the random-arrival wait distribution, and for near-random headways that is close to the information-free error floor (a mean predictor yields MdAPE ≈41% for perfectly regular headways and ≈67% for Poisson-like headways — own derivation below), so further gains must come from conditioning information. Elapsed time since the last observed bus passage is the cheapest such variable and is theoretically valuable exactly when headways are far from exponential (bunched pairs plus long gaps), but no study was found that directly quantifies its improvement for individual-wait prediction; survival/AFT models are proven in adjacent bus travel-time problems.

### Cited Findings
- Seminal random-arrival wait formula E[w] = 0.5·E[h]·(1 + cv_h²) (Osuna & Newell 1972; Newell 1982) — the same quantity as E[H²]/(2E[H]); it assumes random arrivals and adjusts for headway irregularity. [S] — [Singh et al., Imperial College (preprint submitted to TR-C, 2021)](https://spiral.imperial.ac.uk/server/api/core/bitstreams/d3b1d171-cdc7-4a7d-9fe7-38d2cecffc20/content)
- Excess waiting time due to irregularity = half the headway variance divided by mean headway. [W, snippet] — [arXiv 2009.13053](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2009.13053)
- Stockholm trunk lines (planned headway 4–6 min): average actual wait 4 min 12 s, 80% wait < 5 min; average actual wait is **57% longer** than with perfectly regular arrivals — i.e., irregularity alone inflates waits substantially even on high-frequency lines. [M] — [Cats & Loutos 2013, KTH](https://www.transportportal.se/swopec/CTS2013-25.pdf)
- Headway sequences are not independent: in a stop-level AVL/APC study, a bus following closely behind its predecessor was usually followed by a larger headway (longer wait for the next bus) — the empirical basis for conditioning on recent passages. [W, abstract-level] — [Empirical findings of bus bunching distributions (ResearchGate)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/266584951_Empirical_findings_of_Bus_Bunching_Distributions_and_Attributes_Using_Archived_AVL_APC_Bus_Data)
- Bunching is self-reinforcing: an early bus meets fewer waiting passengers, dwells less and widens the gap behind it, while the trailing bus catches up. [M] — [Wikipedia: Bus bunching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bus_bunching)
- Kalman-filter AVL/APC arrival/departure prediction defines the predicted headway as the gap between the last bus's actual arrival and the next bus's predicted arrival — i.e., "last passage" is already a standard state variable. [M] — [Kalman AVL/APC model, J. Public Transportation](https://digitalcommons.usf.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1342&context=jpt)
- Survival (duration) models for bus timing: Sharmila, Velaga & Choudhary (IET ITS 2020) model arrival times at downstream stops with Cox and accelerated-failure-time (AFT; Weibull, log-logistic) models with dwell time and passenger count covariates; AFT outperformed Cox for arrival times and their uncertainty. [M, abstract-level] — [x-mol record](https://www.x-mol.com/paper/1290718741115052032)
- Yu, Wood & Gayah (TR-C 2017): AFT survival models predicted bus travel times about as accurately as linear regression but with smaller prediction variances. [M, abstract-level] — [World Transit Research](https://www.worldtransitresearch.info/research/6307); Mamdoohi et al. (2019) found AFT models more accurate than linear regression for bus travel time. [W, abstract] — [DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/625ff813a6ad4073868f4bbc1f0fe735)
- A log-normal AFT model on pace (travel time per unit distance) handled censored/incomplete GPS trajectories best in a recent TRB study. [W, abstract] — [TRID 2796254](https://trid.trb.org/View/2796254)
- Informal example: Kaplan–Meier estimation of passenger wait time treating unboarded waits as censored, plus Nelson–Aalen hazard and Weibull fit. [W] — [Jupyter notebook](https://nbviewer.jupyter.org/github/elvyna/data-analysis/blob/master/code/python/2018-11-18%20Survival%20analysis%20-%20how%20likely%20to%20get%20in%20bus.ipynb)
- Stockholm RTI study: standard deviation of real-time prediction error grows linearly with remaining waiting time (reliability does not improve in relative terms) — supports outputting a predictive *distribution* whose width scales with horizon. [M] — [Cats & Loutos 2013](https://www.transportportal.se/swopec/CTS2013-25.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Renewal-theory identities (standard probability, own derivation):** for random passenger arrival, wait W has density f_W(w) = S_H(w)/E[H], where S_H is the headway survival function; its mean is E[H²]/(2E[H]) (= current model). Conditional on elapsed time *a* since the last passage with no bus since: P(W > w | a) = S_H(a + w)/S_H(a), and E[W | a] = ∫ₐ^∞ S_H(u) du / S_H(a) (the mean residual life of H at age a).
  - Exponential headways (cv = 1): residual life constant → conditioning on *a* gives no gain.
  - Near-regular headways (cv < 1, increasing hazard): expected remaining wait *falls* as *a* grows.
  - Bunched mixtures (many short gaps + long gaps, cv > 1, decreasing hazard): expected remaining wait *rises* with *a* ("the longer you've waited, the longer you'll wait"), and right after a bus passes there is a non-trivial probability of a bunched follower → bimodal conditional distribution. DTC (35 min mean wait, bunching, long gaps) is likely in this regime, so gains from conditioning could be material — but this must be measured.
- **Error floor without information (own derivation, illustrates why 59% is hard to beat historically):** if W is exponential, a constant predictor equal to the mean gives MdAPE ≈ 67%; the MdAPE-optimal constant (≈0.71 × mean) gives ≈ 63%. If headways are perfectly regular (W uniform on [0, H]), predicting the mean H/2 gives MdAPE ≈ 41% and the optimum (2H/3) gives ≈ 33%. Our 59% sits between these, i.e., the historical model is already near the floor; only conditioning information (elapsed time, chained vehicles, terminal state) can move it substantially. Note also that predicting the mean is not MdAPE-optimal: a lower quantile/median can cut a few points "for free", but better practice is to publish a range (e.g., P25–P75) and score with pinball loss/interval coverage.
- **Method (implementable in ~1–2 days):** from 31 days of GPS, derive stop passage times for every route-direction (interpolate between pings at ~20 s polling); compute empirical headway survival S_H per (stop or stop-segment, route-direction or "useful-route set", hour band, day type) with hierarchical shrinkage to route-level; serve E[W | a] and quantiles of S_H(a + w)/S_H(a). Better: a discrete-time hazard model (logistic regression or LightGBM on minute-level "person-period" rows) with covariates: elapsed time since last passage, whether the last two passages were bunched (< 2–3 min apart), elapsed time since last terminal departure, scheduled departures in the next window, count of vehicles on the opposite direction, hour, day type. Equivalent alternative: AFT/survival GBM (e.g., XGBoost AFT objective).
- **Data needed:** only what the team has (GTFS-RT positions, 31-day history, GTFS stops). Evaluate on a held-out week: MdAPE by "bus visible upstream / not visible", and P10–P90 coverage.
- **Caveat specific to DTC:** "elapsed time since last bus" only counts GPS-visible buses; any bus without a working tracker breaks the renewal chain. Because ground-truth waits are also computed from GPS, the model will be internally consistent, but the 35 min measured mean wait may be overstated if some operating buses are invisible (see Q7 on GPS coverage).

### Gaps
- No study found that empirically measures how much conditioning on elapsed time since the last bus improves individual wait predictions (no A/B or backtest result in the literature retrieved). This must be validated on the team's own data.
- No published empirical headway-distribution fits (lognormal/gamma/mixture) for Delhi; the Feng & Figliozzi and similar AVL papers could not be read in full (403).
- A probabilistic headway prediction with relevance vector machines (reported in snippets as achieving >95% of headways within 95% bands) could not be verified from a primary source.

## 2. Predicting buses NOT yet visible upstream (next-trip/block chaining, terminal departure, layovers, timetable + observed adherence)

### Takeaway
Deployed systems generally fall back to the schedule for trips that have not started, and those are where real-time information adds least value; terminal dispatch irregularity is repeatedly identified as the main source of headway irregularity and bunching. No published study was found that quantifies the accuracy gain from block/vehicle chaining (opposite-direction trip → layover → our trip), so the team would be doing something genuinely under-documented — the method is straightforward with 31 days of GPS and should be validated by backtest.

### Cited Findings
- Stockholm (Cats & Loutos 2013): in 20% of cases the real-time display gave exactly the same remaining wait as the timetable, because the RTI scheme reverts to the schedule "when the next bus has not started yet its trip" (or runs early with an intermediate timing point). Only 10% of arrivals were within a narrow interval of the timetable (exact ± value lost in PDF text extraction), and schedule adherence was strongly skewed toward late arrivals with a long right tail. [M] — [Cats & Loutos 2013](https://www.transportportal.se/swopec/CTS2013-25.pdf)
- Same study: timetable-based expectations considerably underestimate waits; the mean absolute deviation of RTI-based expected waits was less than half that of the timetable; the difference between timetable and RTI expectations equals 30% of the average wait. Predictions are most accurate at timing points (drivers try to adhere there), and error turns negative on the last stretch because drivers arrive early at the end terminal "to prolong their break" — i.e., terminal arrival time ≠ terminal departure time minus a fixed layover. [M] — [Cats & Loutos 2013](https://www.transportportal.se/swopec/CTS2013-25.pdf)
- MBTA engineering: before a driver logs on (or if the vehicle's GPS is not reporting) the system has no better estimate than the schedule. [W, snippet; page returned 403] — [MBTA Tech blog](https://medium.com/mbta-tech/64169f1edeee)
- Stockholm SURE model (Zhang, Ma, Zhang, Ling & Jenelius, *Transportation* 2024): "The dispatch at the terminals is the main source of irregularity for headway delays, which can worsen downstream" (citing Soza-Parra et al. 2021); origin (terminal) delay has a significant positive effect on downstream arrival delays; the authors recommend "enhancing the terminal dispatch punctuality". Next-stop arrival-delay RMSE within 42 s (northbound) and 30 s (southbound), R² 92–99%. [S] — [Zhang et al. 2024, DOI 10.1007/s11116-024-10507-3](https://d-nb.info/1342181905/34)
- TriMet (Feng & Figliozzi): irregular departure headway at the initial stop is the key cause of bus bunching, rather than downstream traffic or demand uncertainty; authors recommend departure-headway control at the initial stop. [M, abstract-level] — [Feng & Figliozzi, AVL/APC bunching analysis](https://core.ac.uk/download/84827068.pdf); [TREC project page](https://trec.pdx.edu/research/project/370/)
- Aviation analogue of block chaining: slack absorbs upstream delay and only the excess beyond the buffer propagates to the next departure. [M] — [arXiv 2512.08197](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.08197). A 2026 flight-delay study found network-propagation features help *before* departure but add negligible value once the actual departure delay is known. [W] — [Zenodo 23957760](https://zenodo.org/records/23957760)
- Neural-network departure prediction on 151 routes predicts departure-time deviations (actual − scheduled) "with an accuracy of under 80 s" (next-stop horizon; self-reported). [W] — [arXiv 2501.10514](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.10514)
- Google BusTr: a model that translates road-traffic forecasts into bus delay predictions, trained on agency real-time feeds and used in cities *without* real-time feeds; −30% MAPE vs. DeepTTE baseline; an anecdotal 10 km New Jersey example stayed within 1.5 min; covers hundreds of cities / buses serving 60 million people. [M] — [BusTr, arXiv 2007.00882](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.00882); [Google Research blog](https://research.google/blog/predicting-bus-delays-with-machine-learning/)
- Swiftly release notes mention a fix to predictions on blocks that have not started service (no details) [W] — [Swiftly release notes](https://www.goswift.ly/release-notes); Swiftly's "Auto-Assigner" matches unassigned vehicles to trips when assignment data is missing [W] — [Swiftly blog](https://www.goswift.ly/blog/on-time-performance-measurement-best-practices).
- TheTransitClock (open source, formerly Transitime) Kalman-filter predictions were piloted at Metro Transit (Minneapolis–St Paul) for one month on >900 buses / 167 routes and compared with a schedule-based method [M, abstract-level] — [Networks and Spatial Economics 2021](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11067-021-09541-w); the deployer reports it outperformed the competition and gave superior predictions during disruptions [W] — [Cambridge Systematics](https://camsys.com/blog/a-pilot-to-enhance-real-time-bus-predictions-for-metro-transit).
- Delhi: the 2023 timetable assumes a flat 15 km/h, while observed evening median bus speed was 8.6 km/h (6–7 pm) rising to 25 km/h after 11 pm; observed evening trips were 23% more than the timetable comparison (different snapshots). [W, unreviewed 6-hour analysis] — [how-Delhi-buses-move](https://github.com/rishabhjain9196/how-Delhi-buses-move). A practitioner quoted in 2026 says published static timetables may not match what runs on the ground. [W] — [Swarajya, Mar 2026](https://swarajyamag.com/reports/delhi-has-the-buses-the-data-and-the-apps-why-cant-you-still-find-your-bus)

### Inferences
- **Why chaining should help Delhi specifically:** with UP/DOWN paired routes, the bus that will next serve a passenger is very often *already visible* — running the opposite direction, or stationary at the far terminal — even when nothing is upstream in the passenger's direction. Treating those vehicles as candidates converts part of the "53% not visible" into "visible with layover uncertainty".
- **Departure recursion (standard scheduling logic):** predicted departure of the next trip = max(scheduled departure if DTC actually dispatches to schedule, predicted terminal arrival + layover), where layover is drawn from an *empirical* distribution (not the GTFS minimum), because drivers may arrive early to extend breaks (Stockholm) and Delhi timetables are unreliable. Then add the existing travel-time model from terminal to the passenger's stop.
- **Terminal-dwell hazard:** for a bus already sitting at the terminal, predict departure from the residual-life of the layover distribution given layover time elapsed so far (same mathematics as Q1). For a scheduled trip whose departure time has passed with no bus seen departing, update P(cancelled) vs P(late) with the same residual-life logic.
- **Competing-risks combination:** candidates i (upstream bus; opposite-direction bus via layover; parked-at-terminal bus; schedule-only trip × P(operates); renewal fallback from Q1) each give an arrival-time survival S_i(t); the next arrival survives as S(t) ≈ Π S_i(t) (assuming independence; adjust for the same vehicle appearing twice). Publish the median and an 80% interval.
- **One-week recipe:**
  1. Day 1 — Rebuild vehicle chains from the 31-day GPS (vehicle_id → ordered trips with route, direction, terminal arrival/departure). Estimate per terminal × hour × day type: layover quantiles; P(next trip is the paired reverse trip) vs. other route/depot; operated-trip ratio vs GTFS; terminal departure offset vs GTFS where trip_ids match.
  2. Day 2 — Diagnose the not-visible cases: for each sampled passenger arrival time, classify the eventual next bus's state at that moment (opposite direction / parked at terminal / not reporting / other variant). This directly gives the ceiling on what chaining can recover.
  3. Days 3–4 — Implement candidate generation + per-candidate distributions + competing-risks merge.
  4. Day 5 — Backtest on a held-out week: MdAPE and % within ±max(2 min, 20%) by candidate type and horizon bucket (0–10, 10–20, 20–30, 30–60, >60 min); P10–P90 coverage; compare with current historical/live split.
- **Expected effect (hypothesis, not literature):** chaining-based predictions should land between the live-upstream error (~22%) and the historical error (~59%), with error dominated by layover spread; the share of cases moved from "historical" to "chained" times the per-case improvement is the overall gain. Measure layover IQR first — if it is a few minutes, gains are large; if layovers are tens of minutes and erratic, the gain shrinks toward the renewal model.

### Gaps
- No published study (academic or vendor) quantifying prediction-accuracy gains from block/vehicle chaining or layover modelling for not-yet-started trips; TheTransitClock source code describing its layover ("wait stop") logic could not be retrieved (404 on the path tried), and the Metro Transit pilot paper's numbers were behind a login.
- No published layover-duration distributions for Indian terminals; no data on DTC terminal departure punctuality or trip-cancellation ("missed trips") rates were found.
- The exact definition of the "secondary delay" departure recursion was seen only in a search summary; its source paper could not be confirmed, so it is presented above as standard logic rather than a cited result.

## 3. Bus bunching and headway prediction methods and their accuracy

### Takeaway
Headway/bunching prediction is a mature research topic (LS-SVM, relevance-vector, seq2seq/LSTM, random forests), but reported accuracies are on high-frequency services, use different metrics, and are not comparable; the robust practical insights are that bunching originates mostly from irregular terminal dispatch and that headway-to-leader features improve ETA. For DTC, bunching matters mainly through the "bunched pair then long gap" pattern, which is best handled inside the Q1/Q2 conditional-wait model rather than as a separate classifier.

### Cited Findings
- Yu et al. (TR-C 2016): least-squares SVM predicts stop-level headways from transit smart-card data (two Beijing routes) over varying horizons and flags bunching from predicted headways. [S, abstract-level] — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0968090X16301747); a secondary thesis cites "95% accuracy" for this model (not verified against the original). [W] — [Univ. of Hertfordshire thesis](https://uhra.herts.ac.uk/id/eprint/15769/1/14111965%20RAHI%20Arsalan%20Final%20Version%20of%20MPhil%20Submission.pdf)
- 2026 preprint: bunching anticipated up to two stops ahead with ≈78% accuracy using random forests and LSTM, without GPS (transaction data only). [W, preprint] — [SSRN 6880258](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=6880258)
- 2026 NYC study: LSTM stop-arrival prediction MAE ≈ 2.02 min vs 2.83 min for a route-aware baseline (28.5% improvement), combined with stop-skipping control to prevent bunching. [M] — [Frontiers in Built Environment 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/built-environment/articles/10.3389/fbuil.2026.1894212/full)
- Bunching concentrates in high-frequency hours/segments and increases downstream; irregular initial departure headway is the key cause (TriMet). [M, abstract-level] — [Feng & Figliozzi](https://core.ac.uk/download/84827068.pdf)
- Terminal dispatch is the main source of headway irregularity; segregated lanes and off-board payment are the most effective measures to reduce headway variability (Soza-Parra et al. 2021, Santiago, as summarised); service overlap increases headway irregularity and waiting time (Diab et al. 2016). [S] — [Zhang et al. 2024](https://d-nb.info/1342181905/34)
- 2024 review classifies bunching literature into mathematical/statistical, machine/deep-learning and simulation approaches. [M] — [Transport Reviews 2024](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/01441647.2024.2313969)
- Delhi, 6 h evening snapshot (21–22 Oct 2026, >6,000 buses): one in eight same-route gaps is under 2 min (~12.5%); a LightGBM arrival model beat a historical-median baseline by 14% on familiar routes and 24% on unseen routes (no absolute MAE reported). [W, unreviewed] — [how-Delhi-buses-move](https://github.com/rishabhjain9196/how-Delhi-buses-move). A separate unvalidated "Delhi Bus Bunching Predictor" combines OTD static schedules with GTFS-RT to flag abnormal headways. [W] — [GitHub](https://github.com/rehanraj80222005-wq/DELHI-BUS-BUNCHING)
- Prediction models for bus state routinely use the travel times of preceding buses as a key input. [S] — [Zhang et al. 2024](https://d-nb.info/1342181905/34); [Jinan case study (ResearchGate)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/260019564_Real-Time_Bus_Arrival_Time_Prediction_Case_Study_for_Jinan_China)

### Inferences
- For a passenger-facing wait predictor, a separate "bunching classifier" is less useful than adding bunching state as features: (i) gap (time and distance) between the candidate bus and its leader on the same route-direction, (ii) leader's recent speed (follower in a bunch dwells less and catches up), (iii) whether the last two passages at the stop were < 2–3 min apart (signals a long gap ahead — Q1).
- Because bunching is seeded at terminals (Feng & Figliozzi; Soza-Parra via Zhang et al.), observed terminal departure gaps between consecutive vehicles are an early predictor of downstream bunching — another reason to model the terminal state (Q2).
- With ~12.5% of Delhi gaps < 2 min (if confirmed on 31 days), a material share of "next bus" events are bunched followers; for those, the useful message to the passenger is the *following* gap, i.e., "next bus in 3 min, then likely ~40 min" — a two-bus display reduces the impact of bunching on perceived reliability.

### Gaps
- No bunching/headway-prediction study for Indian cities with peer-reviewed accuracy figures was found.
- Reported accuracy of Yu et al. (2016) and the relevance-vector headway model could not be verified from full text (paywall/403).
- No study measured how much headway-to-leader features improve ETA accuracy for long-headway (≥20 min) services.

## 4. Real-time ETA accuracy versus horizon in practice (MBTA/WMATA, Transit app, Swiftly, TfL, Google, TheTransitClock, academic) and "ghost bus" handling

### Takeaway
Industry accuracy standards stop at a 30-minute horizon and allow windows that widen with horizon (±1 min at 0–3 min up to −4/+6 min at 12–30 min); well-run US systems score roughly 74–85% "accurate" within those windows, and error spread grows roughly linearly with horizon with a systematic underestimation of long waits. Ghost-bus mitigation in practice means not publishing predictions for cancelled/unassigned trips, separating schedule-based from GPS-based predictions, and measuring "completeness" as well as accuracy.

### Cited Findings
- **Accuracy windows (WMATA, same ranges as MBTA):** 0–3 min out: 1 min early to 1 min late; 3–6 min: 1.5 early / 2 late; 6–12 min: 2.5 early / 3.5 late; 12–30 min: 4 early / 6 late. Accuracy = share of predictions within these windows among all predictions made within 30 min of arrival. [M, official] — [WMATA dashboard definitions](https://www.wmata.com/initiatives/open-datahub/upload/SEDashboardDefinitions_20230626.pdf); [MBTA arrival prediction accuracy](https://www.mbta.com/performance-metrics/arrival-prediction-accuracy)
- **MBTA (older engineering post):** 76% accurate at 0–3 min, 74% at 3–6 min, 78% at 6–12 and 12–30 min; the new vendor was expected to reach 84% based on procurement data. [W, snippet; page 403] — [MBTA Tech blog](https://medium.com/mbta-tech/64169f1edeee)
- **Transit app (2023, spokesperson):** predictions "acceptable" about 92% of the time for rail and 85% for buses (company self-report). [W] — [Billy Penn](https://billypenn.com/2023/08/28/septa-real-time-tracking-app-customer-service-leslie-richards/)
- **Transit + Swiftly ETA Accuracy Benchmark:** more tolerance at longer horizons, much stricter on *early* errors near arrival (a late bus can still be caught, an early one cannot); ghost buses and "% trips with real-time" were listed as future metrics. [W] — [Swiftly blog](https://goswift.ly/blog/how-on-target-is-that-eta-really-now-theres-a-way-to-know); [TransitApp/ETA-Accuracy-Benchmark](https://github.com/TransitApp/ETA-Accuracy-Benchmark/). Swiftly later added an "ETA Completeness Benchmark" measuring whether riders get real-time info at all. [W] — [Swiftly blog](https://www.goswift.ly/blog/prediction-accuracy-more-than-the-best-transit-etas)
- **Vendor improvement claims:** Swiftly reports a 22% accuracy gain and a 94% cut in "miss-the-bus" predictions at one agency [W] — [Swiftly](https://www.goswift.ly/blog/best-in-class-real-time-passenger-information); 30–50% improvement at Pierce Transit [W] — [Swiftly case study](https://www.goswift.ly/blog/how-pierce-transit-used-swiftly-to-improve-prediction-accuracy-by-up-to-50).
- **Stockholm, academic audit (trunk lines, 4–6 min headways):** prediction error ≈ normal with mean +15 s and SD 2 min; 64% within ±1 min; 36% off by >1 min, 14% by >2 min, 5% by >4 min, two-thirds of large errors are underestimates; average underestimation 6.2% per displayed minute (an "SL minute" lasts 63.7 s); short waits overestimated, waits >2 min increasingly underestimated, approaching 10% for waits >8 min (a 9-min display ≈ 10-min wait); error SD rises linearly with remaining wait; worst on Saturdays (SD 2:52) vs Mon/Tue (1:46–1:51) and in PM peak (SD 2:12 vs 1:35 AM); ~5% of displays showed "Now" when the bus had not arrived. [M] — [Cats & Loutos 2013](https://www.transportportal.se/swopec/CTS2013-25.pdf)
- Adding instantaneous downstream travel conditions to the Stockholm scheme reduced MAE by 25% (TR-A 2016). [S, abstract] — [Cats & Loutos 2016, TU Delft](https://research.tudelft.nl/en/publications/evaluating-the-added-value-of-online-bus-arrival-prediction-schem)
- Singapore/London: 75% of predictions within 45 s using real-time data vs 55% using historic records. [W, abstract-level] — [arXiv 1907.08483](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1907.08483)
- WMATA BusETA citizen audit: overall acceptable, buses sometimes earlier than predicted, ghost buses "still real", and accuracy worsens the farther away the bus is. [W] — [Greater Greater Washington](https://ggwash.org/view/41419/metros-bus-app-is-pretty-accurate-but-leave-yourself-some-extra-time)
- **Ghost-bus handling:** CTA attributed ghost buses to mixing scheduled runs with real-time data and changed Bus Tracker to show only GPS-based predictions [M] — [Streetsblog Chicago 2022](https://chi.streetsblog.org/2022/08/24/cta-unveils-improvements-to-bus-tracker-but-will-they-bust-the-ghost-run-problem); Swiftly does not generate predictions for cancelled trips or closed stops to reduce ghost buses [W] — [Swiftly](https://www.goswift.ly/blog/best-in-class-real-time-passenger-information); TfL developers report predictions that disappear/reappear and cancelled buses in the API [W] — [TfL tech forum](https://techforum.tfl.gov.uk/t/buses-that-disappear-from-predictions-api/2108); a Delhi hobby tracker drops GPS pings older than 180 s [W] — [DTC-Tracker](https://github.com/saranshgemini18-del/DTC-Tracker).
- **TheTransitClock:** has horizon-based error analysis and a playback accuracy module, but no published within-1-min percentages were found [W] — [mailing list](https://groups.google.com/g/thetransitclock/c/UIDcSitNJIA); [wiki: Kalman testing](https://github.com/TheTransitClock/transitime/wiki/Prediction-Kalman-testing).
- **Google:** BusTr −30% MAPE vs DeepTTE (see Q2). [M] — [arXiv 2007.00882](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.00882)

### Inferences
- The MBTA/WMATA windows translate to relative tolerances of roughly ±33% at 3 min and −13%/+20% at 30 min; there is **no industry standard beyond 30 min**, yet most DTC waits (mean ≈35 min) sit at 20–60+ min horizons. Propose reporting our own buckets (0–10, 10–20, 20–30, 30–60, >60 min) with an asymmetric window such as −max(2 min, 15%) / +max(2 min, 25%) plus MdAPE, so results are comparable to MBTA at short horizons.
- Stockholm shows systematic *underestimation* of long waits (~10% for >8 min); for DTC the same bias at 30–60 min would mean several minutes. Calibrate with a horizon-dependent bias correction (quantile regression of actual/predicted by horizon bucket) — cheap and usually effective.
- Because early arrivals make passengers miss the bus, optimise and report early-error rates separately; for "leave home at" advice use a lower predictive quantile.
- Ghost-bus controls for DTC GTFS-RT: drop stale pings (>~180 s), flag vehicles stationary far from terminals for long periods, never show schedule-only trips as live; show "scheduled — X% likely to run" using the observed operated-trip ratio from 31 days.

### Gaps
- No official TfL iBus/Countdown accuracy percentages were found.
- No public accuracy-by-horizon numbers for Google Maps transit ETAs, Transit's own predictions beyond the single 85% figure, or TheTransitClock deployments.
- No public benchmark exists for horizons > 30 min, the regime most relevant to DTC.

## 5. Passenger arrival behaviour: headway threshold for timed arrivals, and how to model expected wait

### Takeaway
Empirical thresholds for the switch from random to timed passenger arrivals range from ~2–3 min (London Underground smart-card study) to ~5–11 min (Zurich, Copenhagen, Austin, Manchester), with the US TCQSM using 10 min; at 30–60 min headways most passengers time their arrival if the schedule is reliable. But timing collapses when service is irregular (passengers arrive more randomly when schedule deviation is high), so for DTC's unreliable low-frequency service the random-arrival model is appropriate for passengers without real-time info, while app users can time arrivals to predictions (Seattle: ~2 min less actual wait).

### Cited Findings
- Review of thresholds [S, review within Singh et al.] — [Singh et al., Imperial College](https://spiral.imperial.ac.uk/server/api/core/bitstreams/d3b1d171-cdc7-4a7d-9fe7-38d2cecffc20/content):
  - US Transit Capacity and Quality of Service Manual: transition at 10-min headways (Kittelson & Associates 2013).
  - O'Flaherty & Mangan (1970), Leeds bus: E[w] = 1.79 + 0.14h → transition ~5 min.
  - Seddon & Day (1974), Manchester bus: E[w] = 2.34 + 0.26h → transition ~10 min.
  - Fan & Machemehl (2009), Austin bus, headways 8–60 min: E[w] = 2 + 0.3h; transition at 11 min; beyond 38-min headways all arrivals non-random.
  - Nygaard & Torset (2016), Trondheim bus: non-random at 10, 15, 20-min headways; E[w] = 0.32h for 10-min service.
  - Luethi et al. (2007), Zurich bus/tram/rail: mixed uniform + Johnson SB distribution; transition ~5 min.
  - Ingvardson et al. (2018), Copenhagen metro/regional rail, headways 2–60 min: mixed uniform + beta fits better; non-random share 43% at 5-min and 52% at 10-min headways; metro (frequency-based, no timetable) arrivals more random than timetabled rail.
  - Berggren et al. (2019), Lund/Malmö app data: non-random behaviour already at 5-min headways.
- Singh et al. (London Underground, Oyster AFC + AVL, semiparametric regression): marginal wait falls from > ½ headway to ≈ ⅓ headway; transition at **2–3 min** headways; real-time information is "decisive" around this critical headway. [S] — [Singh et al.](https://spiral.imperial.ac.uk/server/api/core/bitstreams/d3b1d171-cdc7-4a7d-9fe7-38d2cecffc20/content)
- Zurich: even at a 5-min headway in the weekday peak, 59% of passengers timed their arrival. [W, snippet] — [Luethi, Weidmann & Nash 2007](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228915616_Passenger_arrival_rates_at_public_transport_stations)
- Reliability effects: Bowman & Turnquist (1981, Chicago) — passengers more sensitive to headway regularity than to headway; Frumin & Zhao (2012, London Overground) — passengers arrive more randomly when schedule deviation is high; Nygaard & Torset (2016) — waits grow as deviation from schedule grows; four studies find more random arrivals off-peak. Csikos & Currie (2008, Melbourne) — the most consistent regular riders arrive 1–3 min before scheduled service. [S, review] — [Singh et al.](https://spiral.imperial.ac.uk/server/api/core/bitstreams/d3b1d171-cdc7-4a7d-9fe7-38d2cecffc20/content)
- Timing shares used in a 2026 line-planning model: 55%, 74%, 90%, 92% of passengers time their arrival at 10, 20, 30, 60-min headways (secondary citation). [W] — [arXiv 2607.13263](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.13263)
- Real-time information (Seattle OneBusAway; Watkins et al., TR-A 2011): RTI users' actual (usual) wait almost 2 min shorter than schedule-information users; perceived wait reduced by 0.7 min (~13%); RTI users do not perceive waits as longer than measured. [S] — [Watkins et al. 2011 record](https://iaorifors.com/paper/72407); [World Transit Research](https://www.worldtransitresearch.info/research/3939)
- Kigali pilot (HarakaPlus: GTFS + live GPS, app/web/USSD): average passenger waiting times −38% on three routes (company-reported). [W] — [Africa Prize 2026 profile](https://africaprize.raeng.org.uk/2026-cohort/millicent-kariuki/)

### Inferences
- **Benchmark for "how good DTC could be":** with a reliable schedule, Fan & Machemehl's E[w] = 2 + 0.3h implies ≈11 min at a 30-min headway and ≈20 min at 60 min; DTC's measured 35-min random-arrival mean shows that schedule-timing currently gives passengers little benefit, consistent with Frumin & Zhao (irregularity → random arrivals).
- **Which wait to predict:** (a) "I'm at the stop now" → random-arrival conditional wait (Q1/Q2 model) — the right evaluation target; (b) "When should I leave?" → timed arrival; the realised wait = actual bus arrival − (predicted lower quantile − walking time). Report both: expected wait for a random arrival, and expected wait + miss probability for a user who follows the app's leave-by time.
- **Modelling mixed populations (if estimating system-level average waits):** E[w] = p_random·E[H²]/(2E[H]) + (1 − p_random)·E_timed, with p_random rising as reliability falls; with DTC's unreliability, p_random is likely high for non-app users regardless of headway, so the current random-arrival model is defensible as the system-level baseline.

### Gaps
- No smart-card or GPS-based passenger-arrival study for Indian buses was found (DTC fares are largely paper/mobile tickets, so tap-in data may be unavailable).
- No study found that quantifies how far timed arrival degrades as a function of a measured schedule-adherence metric (only qualitative/regression-direction evidence).

## 6. Transfer waits and missed-connection probability in irregular networks

### Takeaway
Empirical missed-transfer risk in a US network (Columbus, COTA) averaged 7–8.5% with a mean time penalty of ~4 min but very high variance, and the penalty grows where the receiving route has long headways — the DTC situation. In irregular low-frequency networks there are few "scheduled" connections to protect, so transfer wait is best predicted as the conditional residual wait of the second route evaluated at the (uncertain) predicted arrival time at the transfer stop.

### Cited Findings
- COTA, Columbus, Ohio (Feb 2018–Jan 2019; GTFS + real-time + APC): Risk of Missing Transfers (RoMT) 7.14% (σ = 25.75%) with Average Total Time Penalty (ATTP) 3.74 min (σ = 12.97) using GTFS real-time; 8.55% (σ = 27.96%) and 4.57 min (σ = 15.44) using merged APC–GTFS; only 45.06% of records matched. ATTP has a significant negative correlation with hourly frequency, RoMT does not (daily-frequency correlation p = 0.38 for ATTP, 0.118 for RoMT); time penalties are larger at night because of longer headways; simulated dedicated bus lanes save 2.25 min and 5.25% risk for transfers from the improved route but only 0.32 min and *increase* risk by 9.03% for transfers into it. [M] — [Liu et al., "Measuring risk of missing transfers…"](https://luyuliu.github.io/files/pdfs/Transfer_Risk.pdf)
- Copenhagen: >1,100 transfers 300S→200S observed at one site from smart-card data (smart card used by only ~30% of passengers); real-time holding heuristic minimises costs including missed connections, built on short-term travel-time prediction with uncertainty handling. [M] — [arXiv 2104.06819](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.06819)
- Service overlap increases headway irregularity and passenger waiting time (Diab et al. 2016, as summarised). [S] — [Zhang et al. 2024](https://d-nb.info/1342181905/34)

### Inferences
- **Method:** for a two-leg trip, sample (Monte Carlo, e.g., 1,000 draws) the arrival time T1 at the transfer stop from the leg-1 predictive distribution (ride-time model + leg-1 wait), add walking/transfer time, then evaluate leg-2 wait from the Q1/Q2 model *as of time T1* (visible candidates on route 2 propagated forward, else renewal residual given elapsed time since route 2's last passage). Output P(catch a specific visible bus on route 2) and the distribution of total door-to-door time. Medians do not add; summing per-leg medians biases door-to-door estimates — convolve distributions instead.
- **Applicability:** this reuses the components already built for Q1/Q2; it should mainly improve door-to-door error for multi-leg trips (current door-to-door MdAPE 34% historical / 25% live), where transfer waits on long-headway routes dominate the variance (COTA shows ATTP rises as frequency falls).
- Flag a transfer as "risky" when P(miss the visible connecting bus) exceeds a threshold (e.g., 30%), and show the expected penalty (the next route-2 residual wait), which for DTC can be 30+ min.

### Gaps
- No study of transfer risk or transfer waits in Indian or other developing-country bus networks was found.
- No production system with real-time missed-connection probabilities for buses was documented in the sources found (only research prototypes and holding-control studies).

## 7. Evidence from India and other developing-country cities (schedule adherence, ETA accuracy)

### Takeaway
Indian research on bus ETAs (mostly IIT Madras, Chennai/Mysore/Surat) focuses on travel-time prediction for buses already running (Kalman filters, time-space discretisation, k-NN, SVR) and reports moderate gains over historical averages; there is almost no published, independently measured ETA accuracy for deployed Indian passenger systems, and Delhi-specific evidence is limited to data-availability reports, a travel-time-variability study and unreviewed community analyses. Latin American and African evidence shows that terminal dispatch irregularity and unpredictable frequencies dominate waits, and that live information can cut waits substantially (company-reported −38% in Kigali).

### Inferences
- Indian ETA literature is almost entirely about buses already in motion (the problem the team's ~22% live model already addresses); the team's gap — buses not yet visible, terminal behaviour, layovers — is essentially unaddressed in published Indian work, so Q1/Q2 methods are the highest-leverage contribution and a credible "novelty" claim for the hackathon.
- Delhi-specific data risks to design around: missing vehicle registrations, offline GPS units, DEVI stops at unmarked points (stop-passage inference must use distance-to-route rather than geofenced stops), and a timetable based on unrealistic speeds (15 km/h vs 8.6 km/h evening median) → recalibrate any schedule-based component from observed data instead of trusting GTFS times.
- The terminal-dispatch finding from Santiago/Stockholm/Portland transfers to Delhi: measuring terminal departure regularity (gap CV between consecutive departures) per route from the 31-day data is both a predictor for the model and an actionable operator KPI.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed, independently measured ETA accuracy (by horizon) for any deployed Indian passenger information system (Delhi, Mysore, Bengaluru, Chennai) was found; Mysore's "90%" is a perception survey.
- Unverified figures from IIT Madras work seen only in search snippets: an "Advanced Time-Space Discretization" method beating historical average by up to 11% (and base TSD by 5%) with max deviation 2–3 min at 10 km ahead, and an Anil Kumar et al. claim of accuracy within (likely) ±5 min for a 30-min window in 92% of instances — original documents not retrieved, so exact sources and values are unconfirmed.
- No quantified data on DTC schedule adherence at terminals, trip cancellations, or ghost-bus frequency in Delhi's GTFS-RT feed.
- No Nairobi, Kampala, Accra, Bogotá or Mexico City ETA-accuracy studies were found; IISc CiSTUP reportedly used RNNs to improve ETA on BMTC route 276 (news report), but no error figures were found.
